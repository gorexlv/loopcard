# LoopCard Web Impeccable Critique

Method: dual-agent (A: impeccable_design_review · B: impeccable_detector_review)

## Design-specificity verdict

**Public-facing experience: distinctly authored. Logged-in product: category-interchangeable.**

The homepage, Market, and deck preview share a coherent editorial world: paper texture, oversized literary serif, restrained dark navy, turquoise memory signal, card-stack compositions, numbered chapters, and language centered on recall. That specificity fades after authentication: the library becomes a conventional sidebar/dashboard, deck creation becomes a plain form and textarea, and study adopts a separate phone-simulator shell.

## Cognitive Load

Four of eight checks fail: chunking, one thing at a time, minimal choices, and working-memory support. Market exposes 11 categories at once. Study exposes reveal, tabs, Save, three ratings, and card toggle in the same state. Card authors must remember delimiter syntax and mentally validate every line.

## Emotional Journey

- Homepage creates intrigue and confidence through a product-specific visual world.
- Deck detail is the trust peak because users can manipulate real cards before commitment.
- Sign-in/import is the main valley: the user wants to learn but is diverted into account and inventory work.
- Study feels focused, then uncertain because ratings have no visible consequence.
- Creation ends administratively with parser syntax and “Deck saved” instead of showing the first new card ready to recall.

## Strengths

1. The public visual system is unusually specific to memory and recall.
2. `MarketDeckPreview` earns trust with real front/back manipulation, layered backs, and a card index.
3. The study stage protects attention and realizes “No feed. No clutter. Just the card.”

## Priority Issues

### P1 — Market discovery does not resolve into a first study session

`apps/web/app/market/[slug]/page.tsx` offers Sign in/Add but no direct Study now continuation. Preserve add intent through authentication and make add-and-begin the default path.

Suggested command: `$impeccable onboard`

### P1 — Study allows invalid learning behavior and gives ratings no consequence

`apps/web/components/study-experience.tsx` renders ratings before reveal, advances irreversibly, stores state only in memory, and cannot undo. Gate rating behind reveal, explain scheduling consequences, persist progress, and add undo/previous.

Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`

### P1 — Card creation is a parser interface, not a card-making experience

`apps/web/components/deck-editor.tsx` uses one textarea with `front :: back`, without structured rows, parsed preview, invalid-line feedback, count, or reordering. Use live structured front/back editing and retain paste as a power-user path.

Suggested command: `$impeccable shape`

### P2 — Workspace and study fracture identity and navigation

`apps/web/app/app/layout.tsx` and `study-experience.tsx` introduce separate shells with no clear continuity or study escape. Establish one authenticated shell and carry the editorial card vocabulary into functional surfaces.

Suggested command: `$impeccable layout`

### P2 — Market delays the task on mobile and overloads category choice

The mobile Market hero pushes search and content below the fold; the catalog exposes 11 categories. Compress the framing, surface search and popular subjects first, and progressively disclose the rest.

Suggested command: `$impeccable adapt`

## Detector Corroboration

CLI scan exited 2 with two findings. `side-tab` is a real 4px accent stripe but has product-specific semantic use and should be downweighted. `codex-grid-background` targets unused `.download-cta` CSS and is a current-UI false positive.

Fresh-browser homepage detection found 39 anti-pattern signals: roughly 31 undersized UI text instances, three low-contrast findings, one text overflow, plus tracking and stylistic advisories. Small functional text, low contrast, and overflow are the actionable findings; generic-style labels are secondary to product context.

## Persona Red Flags

- Jordan (first-timer): “Sign in to add” does not explain whether adding starts study or where the deck goes; delimiter syntax creates avoidable uncertainty.
- Alex (power user): no keyboard rating/navigation, undo, parsed batch validation, or add-and-study shortcut.
- Casey (mobile): Market utility arrives below a tall manifesto; session state is lost on refresh; ratings before reveal invite accidental taps.

## Minor Observations

- Public navigation has no active-page state.
- The mobile details menu has no explicit close behavior after navigation.
- “Save” on a study card is ambiguous and appears non-persistent.
- Interface language is inferred from card CJK content rather than user locale.
- Destructive deck deletion has no confirmation or undo.
- Preview tabs lack complete tabpanel and arrow-key semantics.

## Questions to Consider

- What if selecting a Market deck meant starting the first loop, with saving as a consequence?
- If “one thought at a time” is the product principle, why does creation begin with one textarea containing a whole deck?
- What should each rating visibly change so users can trust the loop?
- Could the authenticated experience feel like opening a personal memory index instead of entering an admin dashboard?
